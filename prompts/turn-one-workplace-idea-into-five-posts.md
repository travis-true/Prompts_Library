# Turn one workplace idea into five posts

**Audience:** Workplace

## Purpose

Turn one workplace, professional-development, business, or thought-leadership idea into five distinct posts tailored to a specific audience, platform, goal, and voice.

## When to use it

Use this prompt when you have one useful idea but need several original posts for LinkedIn, an internal social channel, a professional community, or another business-facing platform.

## Copy-ready prompt

```txt
You are a senior workplace content strategist and copywriter.

GOAL

Turn the supplied idea into five distinct, platform-ready posts. Each post must explore a meaningfully different angle rather than merely rewording the same message.

CONTEXT

Audience: [AUDIENCE]
Platform: [PLATFORM]
Communication goal: [GOAL]
Brand voice and tone: [TONE]
Desired length per post: [LENGTH]
Optional call to action or offer: [CTA OR OFFER]
Hashtags or emojis: [NONE OR REQUIREMENTS]

SOURCE

Core idea: [IDEA]
Supporting facts, examples, experiences, or source material: [SOURCE MATERIAL]

Treat the supplied material as the factual boundary. Do not invent statistics, quotations, outcomes, customer stories, research findings, or personal experiences. If no supporting material is provided, develop the idea using general reasoning without presenting unsupported claims as facts.

EXPECTATION

Before writing, silently identify:
1. The core insight.
2. The audience’s most relevant problem, need, or desired outcome.
3. The strongest tension, misconception, unanswered question, or credible contrarian angle.

Use five different formats or angles selected from:
- Contrarian take
- Personal or practitioner story
- Tactical how-to
- Common mistake
- Myth versus reality
- Data or research insight
- Behind the scenes
- Question-led reflection
- Mini case study
- Prediction

Use a personal story, case study, or data-based angle only when the source material supports it. Otherwise, choose another format.

Each post must include:
- Angle: the selected format or perspective.
- Hook: a specific opening line that earns attention without clickbait.
- Body: useful, concrete, human-sounding value appropriate for the audience and platform.
- Takeaway: one clear point the reader can remember or apply.
- CTA: a natural next step aligned with the platform and communication goal.

Quality requirements:
- Make all five posts substantively different in perspective, structure, or reader value.
- Use concrete details, active voice, and varied sentence lengths.
- Match the requested brand voice without caricaturing it.
- Avoid generic filler and formulaic AI language, including “game-changer,” “unlock,” “dive in,” “in today’s fast-paced world,” “elevate,” “seamless,” “revolutionize,” and “it’s not just X, it’s Y.”
- Do not use hashtags or emojis unless the context explicitly requests them.
- Do not add a preamble, explanation, or writing-process commentary.

Use this output structure:

Post 1
Angle:
Hook:
Body:
Takeaway:
CTA:

Repeat for Posts 2–5.

If missing information would materially affect the posts, ask no more than three focused questions before writing. Otherwise, proceed using the supplied context.
```

## Customize before running

- `[AUDIENCE]`: Intended readers, such as “corporate trainers supporting new managers.”
- `[PLATFORM]`: Publishing destination, such as “LinkedIn” or “Microsoft Teams.”
- `[GOAL]`: Intended response, such as “increase registrations for a webinar.”
- `[TONE]`: Voice characteristics, such as “practical, credible, and conversational.”
- `[LENGTH]`: Approximate size, such as “100–150 words each.”
- `[CTA OR OFFER]`: Desired next step, such as “download the checklist,” or “none.”
- `[NONE OR REQUIREMENTS]`: State “none” or specify permitted hashtags and emojis.
- `[IDEA]`: The central concept to develop.
- `[SOURCE MATERIAL]`: Approved facts, experiences, links, notes, or “none provided.”

## Example output

*Illustrative inputs: Audience: workplace trainers; Platform: LinkedIn; Goal: encourage SOP reviews; Idea: outdated SOPs create hidden rework.*

**Post 1**

**Angle:** Common mistake

**Hook:** Your SOP can be followed perfectly and still produce the wrong result.

**Body:** Teams often review procedures for grammar while overlooking changed systems, roles, and approval paths. A cleaner document is not necessarily a current one. Start each review by testing the procedure against one recent real-world case.

**Takeaway:** Validate the workflow before polishing the wording.

**CTA:** Choose one frequently used SOP and test it this week.

**Post 2**

**Angle:** Tactical how-to

**Hook:** A ten-minute SOP check can expose months of hidden rework.

**Body:** Ask three questions: Does the named system still exist? Does the correct role own each step? Does the final result match today’s standard? Flag any answer that depends on memory or an undocumented workaround.

**Takeaway:** Review systems, ownership, and outcomes—not just sentences.

**CTA:** Save these three questions for your next process review.

**Post 3**

**Angle:** Contrarian take

**Hook:** The longest SOP is rarely the safest SOP.

**Body:** More detail can bury the decision that matters most. Effective procedures make required actions, exceptions, and verification points easy to find. Length helps only when every section supports correct performance.

**Takeaway:** Completeness should improve action, not increase page count.

**CTA:** Find one paragraph that should become a decision step.

**Post 4**

**Angle:** Question-led reflection

**Hook:** What unofficial step does everyone know—but nobody documented?

**Body:** That hidden step is where onboarding slows down and errors multiply. Ask experienced employees what they always explain after handing someone the official procedure. Their answers reveal documentation gaps.

**Takeaway:** Repeated verbal coaching often signals a missing instruction.

**CTA:** Ask one experienced teammate that question today.

**Post 5**

**Angle:** Behind the scenes

**Hook:** The most useful part of an SOP review happens away from the document.

**Body:** Watch someone complete the task. Notice where they pause, switch systems, seek approval, or ignore a written step. Those moments show where the procedure and actual work have separated.

**Takeaway:** Observation reveals gaps that proofreading cannot.

**CTA:** Pair your next document review with one live walkthrough.

## Check before using

- Confirm the audience, platform, goal, and length.
- Supply evidence for statistics, stories, and case studies.
- Verify that each post offers a genuinely different angle.
- Review the CTA for platform fit.
- Remove any unsupported factual claims.
