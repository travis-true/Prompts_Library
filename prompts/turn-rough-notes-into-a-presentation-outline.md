# Turn rough notes into a presentation outline

**Audience:** Workplace

## Purpose

Turn rough notes or reports into a concise PowerPoint outline with takeaway headlines, supporting points, and visual suggestions.

## When to use it

When you have source content for a leadership, client, or stakeholder presentation and need a logical slide sequence before building the deck. This prompt produces an outline; it does not create a PowerPoint file.

## Copy-ready prompt

```txt
Act as an experienced presentation designer and business communications consultant who has built decks for senior executives and investors.

Take the raw content I paste in—whether that’s bullet points, a report, or a stream-of-consciousness brain dump—and restructure it into a clear, logically sequenced slide-by-slide outline ready to build out in PowerPoint.

I’ll be presenting this to [insert audience, e.g., senior leadership, investors, or a client] who care most about outcomes and next steps rather than process detail.

Follow these requirements:

- Each slide should have no more than one core idea.
- Use a strong headline that states the takeaway rather than just the topic.
- Include no more than four supporting bullet points per slide, written in plain, jargon-free language.
- Keep the total deck to 12 slides or fewer unless the content genuinely demands more.
- Use a confident, concise tone throughout. Avoid filler phrases, hedging language, and repetition of points already made elsewhere in the deck.

Structure the output as a numbered slide-by-slide breakdown. For each slide, provide:
1. The headline.
2. Supporting bullet points.
3. A one-line suggestion for any visual, chart, or diagram that would strengthen the slide.

Before finalizing your answer, review it against this checklist:
- Does every slide headline state a takeaway rather than a topic?
- Is the content free of jargon and filler?
- Does the sequence build a logical narrative from problem to solution to next steps?
- Is any slide overloaded with more than one idea?

Revise anything that fails this check before presenting the final version.

Raw content:
[Paste your notes, report, or draft here.]
```

## Customize before running

- **Audience:** Replace the bracketed audience with the actual viewers and their priorities.
- **Raw content:** Paste authorized notes, report excerpts, or a draft. Label facts, proposals, and unknowns clearly.
- **Source boundaries:** Provide only material you are authorized to share. Verify all claims against it; confident wording must preserve meaningful uncertainty.
- **Presentation context:** Include the purpose, available presentation time, and any required next action in your notes.

## Example output

*Fictional example; illustrates the requested structure.*

Input: A service team’s approved notes identify inconsistent ticket handoffs as a recurring issue. The notes propose a two-week checklist pilot and a follow-up review. No results or owner have been confirmed.

1. **Inconsistent handoffs create a recurring service issue**
   - Approved team notes identify inconsistent ticket handoffs.
   - Baseline frequency and impact have not been measured.
   - Visual: a simple handoff diagram showing where information is missing.
2. **A two-week checklist pilot would test a consistent handoff**
   - Trial a shared checklist for required handoff information.
   - Treat improvements as a hypothesis until reviewed.
   - Visual: a proposed checklist mockup.
3. **Confirm ownership and review criteria before starting the pilot**
   - Assign a pilot owner; none has been confirmed.
   - Agree on evidence to collect and review after two weeks.
   - Visual: a short next-steps table with unconfirmed fields marked.

## Check before using

- Each slide contains one core idea and a takeaway headline.
- Each slide has no more than four supporting bullets and a relevant visual suggestion.
- The outline has 12 slides or fewer unless more are justified.
- The sequence supports the audience’s purpose and ends with appropriate next steps.
- Facts, figures, owners, dates, and commitments match supplied sources; unknowns remain explicit.
- Concise language preserves uncertainty and does not imply proposals are approved or results are proven.
