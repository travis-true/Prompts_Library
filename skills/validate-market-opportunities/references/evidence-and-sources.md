# Evidence and source rules

## Evidence tiers

### Tier A — decision-grade

- First-party store analytics, sales, checkout starts, reviews, and customer messages
- Marketplace transactions, dated reviews, paid demand, procurement notices, budgets, or requests for proposals
- Laws, regulations, official deadlines, government guidance, and authoritative primary research
- Repeated direct buyer requests with clear urgency or willingness to pay

### Tier B — strong supporting evidence

- Representative surveys from credible research organizations
- Current marketplace prices, review volume, sales proxies, and competitor offer density
- Established trade-association reports and platform adoption data
- Multiple independent complaints describing the same job and consequence

### Tier C — exploratory signal

- Search-result volume, social engagement, newsletters, trend roundups, and vendor claims
- Individual forum posts, anecdotes, unverified statistics, and creator speculation

Never recommend Build now using only Tier C evidence.

## Buyer-intent gate

Pass only when all are true:

1. At least two independent evidence sources support the opportunity.
2. At least one Tier A hard signal exists: spending, transactions, purchase request, procurement, binding deadline, or first-party funnel behavior.
3. A repeated complaint or urgent job is documented.
4. A reachable buyer and viable channel are named.
5. The gap closer is materially different from free information already available.

If no Tier A signal exists, assign Validate first or Monitor. If only interest signals exist, reject the commercial build and optionally add it to the passion-project backlog.

## Time windows

- 30-day momentum: new laws, guidance, launches, buyer questions, fresh listings/reviews, rising complaint volume, or new platform changes.
- 90-day validation: repeated signals across sources, sustained buyer activity, recurring purchases, procurement, or durable urgency.
- 12-month context: seasonality, competitive growth, policy trajectory, price stability, and prior spikes that faded.

Do not claim quantitative search growth without a reliable trend dataset.

## Source map

Use the smallest relevant source set.

| Need | Preferred sources |
|---|---|
| Laws and compliance | Official government sites, Federal Register, state agencies |
| Marketplace demand | Etsy, Gumroad, Payhip, Udemy, Maven, marketplace search and dated reviews |
| Complaints | Dated marketplace reviews, Reddit communities, professional forums, public buyer questions |
| Corporate adoption | Microsoft adoption guidance, Gallup, LinkedIn, credible L&D research, buyer communities |
| Competition | Product pages, offer pages, pricing pages, previews, review sections |
| First-party performance | Store analytics, inventory exports, sales reports, traffic and checkout funnels |

## Complaint record

Capture the complaint, buyer role, context, product/category, date, URL, workaround, consequence, repetition, evidence tier, and confidence. Avoid quoting more than necessary. Treat complaints as qualitative evidence unless the source provides a valid count.

## Claim labels

- **Verified fact:** directly supported by cited evidence.
- **Estimate:** a stated calculation or range with assumptions.
- **Inference:** a reasoned conclusion drawn from facts; name the supporting facts.
- **Unknown:** insufficient evidence; do not fill the gap with a guess.
