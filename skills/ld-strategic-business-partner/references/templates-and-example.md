# Stakeholder templates and worked example

## Meeting brief

- **Request and decision needed** — State the deliverable the stakeholder requested and the choice the meeting needs to resolve.
- **Business priority and target workflow** — Describe who needs to do what and why it matters.
- **Evidence and gaps** — Summarize what has been observed or reported and what remains to confirm.
- **People** — Identify the decision-maker, outcome owner, enablement owner, and relevant partners. Roles may overlap.
- **Questions and next step** — Ask what currently happens, what gets in the way, and what success would look like. Propose a next step appropriate to the evidence.

## Options and conversation wording

| Option | Expected benefit | Effort and tradeoffs | Dependencies and owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| Smallest viable response | What it could solve | What it takes and where coverage is limited | Conditions and proposed owner |
| Targeted or broader response | What extra benefit it could provide | Extra effort and what it may displace | Conditions and proposed owner |

Add a third option when it changes the choice. Name the recommendation, why it fits, and the decision needed from the sponsor. Use wording like “We can cover this workflow with a pilot if your team can supply participants and confirm the success criteria. A department-wide rollout would take more preparation and require us to revisit current priorities. Which outcome is most urgent for your team?”

## Decision record

- **Decision and status** — Proposed or agreed, decision-maker, date if known, and scope.
- **Business outcome and rationale** — What should change and why this option fits the evidence.
- **Commitments** — Action, owner, and timing for each party.
- **Review** — Who reviews what evidence, when, and what would justify a change.
- **Open items** — Unconfirmed dependencies, access, baseline, thresholds, or capacity conflicts.

## Worked example: department-wide Copilot training request

**Fictional scenario.** A department sponsor asks for Copilot training for all staff. The sponsor reports inconsistent meeting follow-up. No baseline, capacity estimate, or account entitlements are supplied.

**Meeting brief.** Ask which meeting outputs fall short, who owns follow-up quality, and whether participants can access the intended workflow with their work accounts. Ask the sponsor to bring a de-identified example of an acceptable handoff and confirm the first audience.

| Option | When it fits | Tradeoff and dependency |
|---|---|---|
| Reuse guidance with office hours | Staff can access the workflow and mainly need a refresher | Light preparation. Limited evidence of independent application. Check that the guidance fits the available accounts. |
| Pilot one meeting follow-up workflow | A role has a recurring problem and a manager can sponsor a short trial | Moderate preparation and a narrower audience. Requires approved fictional practice data, access verification, and a review owner. |
| Roll out department-wide | Several roles have verified needs and the sponsor can prioritize the work | Highest coordination effort. Confirm capacity, access across roles, and which work would move. |

**Proposed decision.** Pilot with one team if the sponsor confirms the task, participants, and success criteria and the platform owner verifies access. The specialist facilitates and gathers a fictional practice result. The sponsor reviews whether participants can independently produce an accurate action list against agreed criteria and checks for workplace use when participants have had a realistic opportunity. Review together before expansion. If access blocks the work, route it to the platform owner and revisit the pilot scope. If evidence remains limited, agree what else to collect.
