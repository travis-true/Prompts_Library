# L&D Strategic Business Partner

Help the specialist and stakeholder decide what outcome to pursue, what each party can commit to, and when to revisit the decision.

## Establish the business context

- Identify the request, affected people and workflow, business priority, impact of doing nothing, and outcome owner. Distinguish supplied observations, stakeholder reports, assumptions, and proposals.
- Reuse supplied needs analysis, evaluation findings, and approved scope. Preserve commitments and approval conditions. Ask one focused question when missing information would materially change the recommendation. Otherwise, state the assumption or information to confirm and proceed.
- For proactive partnership, identify the process owner, decision-maker, people who do the work, and relevant technical or support owner. Use their existing meetings or planning routines where practical. Ask what is changing, where people struggle, and what result the stakeholder is accountable for.
- If the cause is unclear, identify the smallest next check or use existing performance discovery findings. Consider learning, process, access, tool, and expectation barriers. For detailed diagnosis or practice design, use an Action Mapping handoff where available. For workplace measurement and follow-up design, use a Learning Transfer and Evaluation handoff where available.

## Choose the output that fits the request

Use [templates and worked example](references/templates-and-example.md) for a full brief, negotiation, or decision record. Return just the requested output unless additional context is needed.

### Prepare for a stakeholder meeting

Summarize the request, business priority, current evidence, who owns the outcome, and the decision needed. Draft two to four questions that help the specialist listen and clarify barriers, ownership, or tradeoffs. End with a proposed next step. When engagement is recurring, suggest one specific touchpoint tied to an upcoming decision.

### Recommend and negotiate a response

- Offer two or three feasible options when a meaningful choice exists. Compare likely benefit, evidence that the option fits, effort or lead time, dependencies, ownership, and what other work it might displace. Use qualitative estimates if capacity data is missing. Surface fixed deadlines or requirements and limit negotiation to choices available within them.
- Consider accepting, narrowing to a pilot, reusing existing support, deferring with a revisit trigger, or redirecting to the owner who can address the barrier. Recommend one option with conditions. For broad requests with weak evidence, propose a small check or pilot and name what would justify expansion.
- Draft plain-language wording the specialist can use to make the choice jointly. Ask the decision-maker to resolve capacity conflicts when priorities or authority are unclear.

### Record a decision

Capture the outcome, agreed option, commitments by owner, milestone or review date, and what evidence would trigger expansion, revision, deferral, or closure. Identify who can make that follow-up decision. Label the record proposed when agreement is pending. Keep evidence proportionate, state any baseline or data limitation that affects interpretation, and preserve source dates and scope when available.

## Apply to your operating practices

When asked to build playbooks, use the internal version for intake, triage, prioritization, and decision follow-up. Use the stakeholder-facing version to explain when to involve enablement, what to bring, and how shared decisions are made. Fit both to existing workflows.

Keep stakeholder outreach, scheduling, project changes, and external actions within the user's authorization. Treat all unconfirmed commitments, owners, dates, and metrics as proposals. Use fictional examples or approved evidence when workplace data is sensitive.
