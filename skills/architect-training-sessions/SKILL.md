# End-to-End Training Session Architect

Design and govern the complete training-session system. Enter at the earliest unfinished phase, preserve approved work, and connect every decision to the workplace performance need.

## Governing rules

- Begin with the required workplace behavior, decision, or outcome—not the slide deck.
- Diagnose whether training can address the need before designing a session.
- Reuse approved, suitable work. Do not reopen, rewrite, or regenerate completed phases without a documented reason.
- Allow entry at any phase and permit phases to be skipped when their required evidence already exists.
- Use only supplied or authoritative sources for process, policy, system, technical, or compliance content.
- Label facts, assumptions, inferences, recommendations, gaps, risks, and decisions.
- Never invent approval, validation, attendance, assessment results, ownership, publishing status, or delivery evidence.
- Make practice resemble the target work closely enough to support transfer.
- Provide feedback where learners must build skill or judgment.
- Prefer point-of-need support over memorization for detailed or infrequent tasks.
- Apply adult-learning, UDL, accessibility, privacy, classification, and platform requirements throughout—not as a final add-on.
- Recommend the smallest effective session and asset system.
- Stop at each material approval gate unless the user authorizes the next phase or the full build.

## Operating modes

Choose the mode that matches the request:

1. **Architecture only:** Produce the strategy, blueprint, decisions, gaps, and plan.
2. **Architecture plus production plan:** Add roles, schedule, dependencies, asset specifications, QA, and release gates.
3. **End-to-end coordination:** Continue through development, readiness, delivery support, reinforcement, evaluation, and closure.
4. **Phase-specific continuation:** Start from an existing approved artifact or completed phase and build only what remains.

If unclear, default to architecture only.

## Workflow

### 1. Establish the project boundary

Determine:

- business need and desired workplace performance;
- audience, roles, experience, access, language, and constraints;
- session type, delivery mode, duration, scale, and timing;
- available sources, existing assets, prior decisions, and approval status;
- technology, venue, facilitator, producer, and support environment;
- information classification, privacy, accessibility, branding, and publishing requirements;
- success measures, decision owners, maintenance needs, and deadlines.

Ask three to five grouped questions when missing answers could materially change the architecture. Target roughly 95% confidence when questions are permitted. If the user requests immediate work, proceed with explicit assumptions and confidence limits.

### 2. Diagnose the need

Confirm:

- the observable behavior or decision that must change;
- the current and desired performance;
- evidence of the gap;
- root causes and barriers;
- what learners already know or can do;
- what training can and cannot address.

If the dominant cause is access, workflow, policy, incentives, staffing, environment, or system design, route the primary intervention appropriately and use training only for the learnable portion.

### 3. Locate the entry phase

Assess existing work against the lifecycle in [references/lifecycle-and-gates.md](references/lifecycle-and-gates.md).

For every phase, classify:

- `COMPLETE AND APPROVED`
- `COMPLETE WITH CONDITIONS`
- `PARTIAL`
- `NOT STARTED`
- `NOT APPLICABLE`
- `NOT VERIFIED`

Start at the earliest phase whose missing evidence prevents reliable downstream work. Preserve completed artifacts as governing inputs.

### 4. Define performance and learning outcomes

Write outcomes that specify:

- audience;
- observable action or decision;
- conditions, tools, and environment;
- expected quality, accuracy, speed, or standard;
- evidence that demonstrates success.

Separate:

- business outcome;
- workplace performance outcome;
- session learning outcomes;
- transfer and adoption outcomes.

Do not use vague verbs as the sole outcome when performance matters. Align assessment and practice to the performance outcome, not merely to content recall.

### 5. Select the session architecture

Choose the shortest defensible learning path. Read [references/session-patterns.md](references/session-patterns.md) when selecting or adapting a format.

Build an intentional sequence from applicable components:

1. orientation and relevance;
2. prior-knowledge activation;
3. concise explanation or modeling;
4. demonstration;
5. guided practice;
6. independent or collaborative application;
7. feedback and debrief;
8. verification or knowledge check;
9. commitment, transfer, support, and close.

Do not force every component into every session. Spend the largest share of available time on application when skill or judgment is the goal.

### 6. Design for variability and access

Provide:

- clear structure, outcomes, instructions, timing, and success criteria;
- multiple useful ways to access essential information;
- alternatives for participation when the primary method creates a barrier;
- accessible documents, visuals, demonstrations, media, activities, and technology;
- captions, transcripts, descriptions, readable contrast, semantic structure, and keyboard access where applicable;
- psychologically safe practice and non-punitive formative feedback;
- manageable cognitive load, progressive complexity, and purposeful breaks;
- examples that reflect actual roles without exposing protected information.

Do not lower the performance standard when offering an equivalent access route.

### 7. Build the content and activity map

For each segment, specify:

- purpose and linked outcome;
- content and authoritative source;
- facilitator action;
- learner action;
- method and materials;
- time;
- evidence or feedback;
- accessibility and technology considerations;
- transition and contingency.

Remove content that does not support an outcome, prerequisite, safety control, or transfer need.

### 8. Route the asset package

Identify which assets must be reused, revised, created, or retired. Typical categories include:

- session blueprint, agenda, and run of show;
- presentation and demonstration materials;
- facilitator or producer guide;
- participant workbook, activity, scenario, or practice files;
- job aid, QRG, checklist, decision guide, or support resource;
- assessment, evaluation, observation, or feedback tool;
- invitations, reminders, manager reinforcement, and follow-up;
- accessibility, publishing, QA, handoff, and maintenance records.

When available, use the companion training-asset recommendation skill for complex routing. Do not create every listed asset by default.

### 9. Define roles, decisions, and handoffs

Read [references/roles-and-handoffs.md](references/roles-and-handoffs.md). Assign only roles the project needs.

For each material deliverable or decision, identify:

- accountable owner;
- responsible producer;
- required reviewer or SME;
- final approver;
- inputs and dependencies;
- acceptance criteria;
- handoff evidence;
- due date and escalation.

Never treat consultation, contribution, or facilitation as approval authority unless explicitly assigned.

### 10. Plan production and control change

Create a dependency-aware plan covering:

- source readiness;
- design decisions;
- asset production;
- reviews and revisions;
- accessibility and technical QA;
- pilot or rehearsal;
- approval and publishing;
- delivery preparation;
- follow-up and measurement;
- maintenance and retirement.

Record material scope, source, audience, duration, platform, policy, or technology changes. Revisit only affected downstream decisions.

### 11. Prepare delivery

Confirm:

- facilitator and producer readiness;
- participant access and prerequisites;
- venue, platform, permissions, links, files, and backups;
- demonstration accounts and fictional or permitted data;
- timing, transitions, interaction setup, and breaks;
- accessibility accommodations and equivalent participation routes;
- support, escalation, incident, and recovery procedures;
- final approved versions and distribution controls.

Use a rehearsal or pilot when complexity, risk, technology, scale, or facilitator readiness justifies it.

### 12. Support delivery

During the session:

- protect the approved scope and timing;
- observe comprehension and performance evidence;
- adapt pacing or support without changing required standards;
- record questions, defects, barriers, deviations, and unresolved items;
- avoid claiming effectiveness from attendance or engagement alone;
- preserve required evidence without collecting unnecessary sensitive data.

### 13. Reinforce and evaluate

After delivery:

- provide approved point-of-need resources and support paths;
- use manager or peer reinforcement only where ownership and expectations are clear;
- space reminders or practice when retention and adoption require it;
- evaluate only against defined decision questions;
- distinguish reaction, learning, behavior, and operational results;
- document limitations, waiting time, confounding factors, and evidence quality.

Do not add surveys or assessments without an intended decision and owner.

### 14. Close, hand off, and maintain

Produce the required handoff:

- approved production baselines;
- source and decision records;
- asset inventory and publishing locations;
- ownership, support, review, and maintenance responsibilities;
- evaluation evidence and limitations;
- open conditions, defects, risks, and deferred work;
- change triggers, regression needs, and retirement criteria.

Set the project status to `CLOSED`, `CLOSED WITH CONDITIONS`, `PAUSED`, or `NOT AUTHORIZED`. Never imply operational authorization from delivery completion alone.

## Required outputs

Use [references/output-templates.md](references/output-templates.md) and produce only what the selected mode and entry phase require. Always include:

- current-state and entry-phase assessment;
- recommended session architecture;
- assumptions, gaps, risks, and decisions;
- approval gate;
- next authorized action.

## Quality gate

Before releasing an architecture or plan, verify:

- traceability to authoritative sources and approved decisions;
- alignment among performance outcomes, learning outcomes, activities, practice, and evidence;
- realistic timing and cognitive load;
- meaningful learner action rather than presentation-only delivery;
- equivalent access and accessibility;
- clear ownership, approvals, dependencies, and handoffs;
- contingency and support for the delivery environment;
- transfer support beyond the event where needed;
- measures connected to defined decisions;
- controlled use of existing approved work.

Use [references/test-cases.md](references/test-cases.md) to validate revisions to this skill.
