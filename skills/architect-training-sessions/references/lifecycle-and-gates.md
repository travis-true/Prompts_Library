# Training session lifecycle and gates

## Contents

1. Lifecycle phases

3. Gate rules

## 1. Lifecycle phases

| Phase | Purpose | Minimum evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0. Intake and authorization | Define request, authority, scope, constraints, and intended decision | Intake brief, owner, scope, source access, authorization to proceed |
| 1. Performance diagnosis | Determine desired performance, current gap, causes, audience, and whether training fits | Performance statement, evidence, cause assessment, non-training needs |
| 2. Learning strategy | Define outcomes, modality, level, practice, support, and measurement approach | Outcome map, architecture decision, constraints, approval |
| 3. Session blueprint | Convert strategy into agenda, segment flow, methods, timing, and evidence | Blueprint, content/activity map, time model |
| 4. Asset strategy | Select the smallest effective learner, facilitator, media, support, and evaluation package | Asset map, reuse/revise/create decisions, specifications |
| 5. Development | Produce and integrate approved content and assets | Drafts, traceability, reviews, defect records |
| 6. Readiness and QA | Verify content, accessibility, technology, facilitation, logistics, and contingency | QA evidence, rehearsal/pilot results, resolved blockers |
| 7. Authorization and release | Approve final baselines and authorize delivery | Final versions, named approval, distribution controls |
| 8. Delivery | Facilitate the approved experience and capture evidence and issues | Attendance if needed, delivery record, deviations, questions, incidents |
| 9. Reinforcement and support | Help learners transfer and sustain performance | Follow-up resources, support, manager or peer actions |
| 10. Evaluation and closure | Answer decision questions, hand off ownership, and set maintenance | Evidence, limitations, decisions, handoff, review triggers |

## 2. Evidence required to skip a phase

A phase may be skipped only when:

- its required evidence exists;
- the evidence is current and applicable;
- approval status is known;
- no unresolved upstream change invalidates it;
- downstream users can access and interpret it.

An artifact’s existence does not prove the phase is complete. A slide deck alone does not establish diagnosis, strategy, practice, readiness, or evaluation.

## 3. Gate rules

At each gate, report:

- decision requested;
- accountable authority;
- evidence reviewed;
- status;
- conditions and deadline;
- downstream work authorized;
- work explicitly not authorized.

Recommended statuses:

- `APPROVED`
- `APPROVED WITH CONDITIONS`
- `REVISE`
- `PAUSED`
- `NOT AUTHORIZED`
- `NOT VERIFIED`

Do not infer approval from silence, attendance, file publication, or prior delivery.

## 4. Change routing

Route change to the earliest affected phase:

| Change | Revisit |
| --- | --- |
| Business outcome or audience | Diagnosis and all affected downstream work |
| Source, policy, system, or workflow | Content map, activities, assets, QA, and release |
| Duration or modality | Blueprint, timing, interaction, accessibility, technology, and assets |
| Facilitator or platform | Delivery design, guides, rehearsal, and contingency |
| Assessment purpose | Outcome alignment, evidence design, privacy, and reporting |
| Brand or format | Affected production and QA only |
| Minor wording with unchanged meaning | Content QA and affected artifacts |

Do not reopen unrelated approved decisions.
