# Session architecture patterns

Select and adapt a pattern; do not force content into a preset structure.

## Brief awareness session

Use for a narrow change, concept, or required action.

- relevance and change;
- essential information;
- example or demonstration;
- required action and support;
- concise check or question.

Avoid using this pattern for complex skill or judgment development.

## Demonstration and guided practice

Use for observable procedures or software tasks.

- orient to the workplace task;
- model the complete task;
- break down critical steps and decisions;
- guide practice with feedback;
- provide independent practice;
- verify performance;
- give a point-of-need reference.

## Scenario-based decision workshop

Use for judgment, exceptions, communication, or problem solving.

- clarify decision standard and constraints;
- examine a realistic scenario;
- make and defend a decision;
- compare consequences and evidence;
- debrief against authoritative criteria;
- apply to a new scenario;
- identify escalation and support.

## Collaborative working session

Use when participants must create, analyze, or decide together.

- define output, roles, evidence, and decision authority;
- provide a shared model or example;
- complete structured work cycles;
- review against criteria;
- resolve or log open decisions;
- assign owners and next actions.

Do not call an ungoverned brainstorming meeting “training.”

## Train-the-trainer

Use when facilitators must deliver or support the learning consistently.

- master the target performance;
- understand learning architecture and boundaries;
- rehearse delivery and demonstrations;
- practice facilitation, feedback, and recovery;
- calibrate evaluation and escalation;
- verify delivery readiness.

## Webinar

Use for scaled explanation, demonstration, or awareness with limited practice.

- define realistic interaction limits;
- use concise engagement to support attention and diagnosis;
- provide post-event practice or support when performance is expected;
- do not claim mastery from attendance or poll responses.

## Hybrid session

Design one coherent experience across locations.

- give remote and in-room participants equivalent access to content, participation, questions, practice, and support;
- assign facilitation and production responsibilities;
- avoid side conversations or materials unavailable to one group;
- test audio, camera, captions, chat, collaboration tools, and contingencies.

## Self-paced plus live session

Use self-paced work for common foundations and live time for application.

- define prerequisites and completion evidence;
- avoid repeating prerequisite content live;
- offer recovery for participants who lack access or completion;
- use live time for practice, coaching, decisions, and feedback.

## Timing principles

- Estimate interaction, transitions, technology, questions, and breaks—not just presentation time.
- Protect practice and debrief before adding optional content.
- Reduce scope when the duration cannot support the required performance.
- Include buffer for complex demonstrations or unfamiliar platforms.
- State whether timing is estimated, tested, or verified.
