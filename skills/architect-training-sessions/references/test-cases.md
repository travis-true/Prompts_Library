# Validation test cases

## Case 1: New session from inception

**Request:** Build a 90-minute virtual session for new supervisors who must conduct a documented performance conversation.

**Expected behavior:** Begin with performance diagnosis and authority, define observable outcomes, include modeling and realistic practice, route supporting assets, establish privacy and escalation controls, and stop at the requested gate.

## Case 2: Approved deck already exists

**Request:** The 60-minute deck is approved. Create the remaining session system without changing it.

**Expected behavior:** Classify the deck as approved input, inspect what lifecycle evidence it covers, preserve it, and build only missing practice, facilitation, reference, readiness, reinforcement, or evaluation components.

## Case 3: Session too short

**Request:** Teach a complex, high-risk workflow with decisions and independent practice in 15 minutes.

**Expected behavior:** Challenge the scope-duration mismatch, protect required practice and verification, and recommend reduced scope, prerequisite learning, or a different architecture.

## Case 4: Non-training cause

**Request:** Employees understand the workflow but cannot complete it because permissions are missing.

**Expected behavior:** Route the primary intervention to access remediation and avoid designing training as the fix.

## Case 5: Hybrid delivery

**Request:** Run one session for 20 people in a room and 30 joining remotely.

**Expected behavior:** Design equivalent participation, assign facilitator and producer functions, test audio/captions/tools, prevent location-based advantages, and include recovery plans.

## Case 6: Webinar mistaken for mastery

**Request:** A 500-person webinar should certify that attendees can perform the task.

**Expected behavior:** Reject attendance as competency evidence, define the webinar’s realistic purpose, and add a defensible performance-verification route if certification is required.

## Case 7: Conflicting sources

**Request:** Design mandatory training from an approved policy and an older procedure that disagree.

**Expected behavior:** Preserve the conflict, block affected content production, identify the accountable resolver, and continue only with unaffected architecture.

## Case 8: Existing session needs improvement

**Request:** Learners liked the session but still cannot perform the task.

**Expected behavior:** Diagnose transfer, practice, feedback, environment, and support gaps; do not treat positive reaction data as learning evidence.

## Case 9: User starts at asset phase

**Request:** The session design and deck are complete; create a participant workbook and job aid.

**Expected behavior:** Verify sufficient upstream evidence, preserve the existing architecture, route to the appropriate asset-building skills, and avoid restarting intake without cause.

## Case 10: Immediate plan with missing data

**Request:** Produce a plan now without asking questions; audience and topic are known, but duration, modality, sources, and owners are not.

**Expected behavior:** Produce a provisional architecture, label assumptions and blockers, lower confidence, and avoid false approval or readiness.

## Blocking failures

The skill fails validation if it:

- begins with slides instead of required performance;
- redesigns approved work without cause;
- invents sources, content, ownership, approval, delivery, or evaluation evidence;
- forces every phase or asset into every project;
- treats attendance, engagement, satisfaction, or confidence as performance proof;
- omits practice and feedback when skill or judgment is required;
- ignores point-of-need support for detailed or infrequent tasks;
- adds assessments or surveys without a decision purpose;
- ignores accessibility, privacy, classification, technology, or contingency;
- authorizes delivery despite unresolved high-risk source or readiness failures.
