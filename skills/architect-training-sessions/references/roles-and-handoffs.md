# Roles, decision rights, and handoffs

Assign functions, not titles. One person may hold several functions when conflicts are controlled.

| Function | Typical accountability |
| --- | --- |
| Business or performance owner | Defines desired result and accepts operational outcome |
| Content owner | Owns authoritative content, scope, currency, and maintenance |
| Subject-matter expert | Supplies and validates domain knowledge within assigned authority |
| Learning architect or instructional designer | Designs outcomes, sequence, methods, practice, and evidence |
| Project or production lead | Coordinates plan, dependencies, status, risks, and handoffs |
| Asset producer | Builds assigned presentation, document, media, or activity |
| Facilitator | Delivers, adapts within authority, supports practice, and records issues |
| Producer or technical moderator | Manages platform, access, interactions, recording, and recovery |
| Accessibility reviewer | Verifies equivalent access and accessibility requirements |
| Privacy, security, legal, policy, or compliance reviewer | Reviews within assigned risk authority |
| Communications or change lead | Coordinates invitations, launch, reinforcement, and stakeholder messages |
| Evaluator or analyst | Designs evidence collection and interprets results for named decisions |
| Publisher or platform administrator | Publishes controlled files and manages access and versions |
| Final approver | Authorizes the defined baseline and permitted next use |

## Handoff minimum

Every material handoff should state:

- deliverable and version;
- source and decision baseline;
- responsible sender and accountable recipient;
- acceptance criteria;
- open conditions, defects, and risks;
- permitted and prohibited next actions;
- due date and escalation;
- evidence of acceptance.

## Decision-right rules

- Approval authority must be explicit.
- A contributor does not approve by contributing.
- An SME validates content only within assigned authority.
- A facilitator may adapt delivery but not silently change required policy or process.
- A producer may fix formatting or technical defects but not alter meaning without content review.
- A final approver authorizes only the scope presented.
- Unresolved high-risk issues block release even when the schedule is threatened.

## Lightweight staffing

For low-risk work, combine functions while retaining:

- one accountable business/content owner;
- one person responsible for learning design and production;
- one qualified reviewer;
- one final approver where approval is required;
- one named facilitator/support route.

Do not create ceremonial roles that add no decision or control value.
