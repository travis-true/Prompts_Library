# Shot analysis

Use timestamps relative to the provided media. Identify whether timings were measured or estimated. Inspect adjacent frames before declaring a cut.

Record occlusion, entrances/exits, gaze, expression, hand/object contacts, screen direction, and foreground/background relationships when important. Treat a zoom inferred from sparse frames as uncertain: a crop, dolly, or subject movement may produce similar changes.

Evidence labels:
- Observed: directly visible or audible in inspected material.
- Inferred: interpretation consistent with evidence; name the basis.
- Proposed: creative or production choice for the new result.
- Unknown: unavailable or not determined.

Express motion with observable verbs, direction, pace, and end state. Prefer “the person turns toward the doorway, pauses, then lowers the cup” over “cinematic dynamic motion.” Do not invent microsecond timing or precise lens values.

For fast movement, inspect dense samples or continuous playback. If only sparse frames are available, explicitly withhold conclusions about motion smoothness, exact transitions, and lip synchronization. Use transcript text only for speech content whose source is identified; it does not verify speaker identity or delivery.
