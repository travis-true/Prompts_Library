# Production brief template

Use only sections needed by the request. For complete projects, preserve the following structure.

## Goal and priorities
- Purpose/audience: [intended result]
- Mode: [close visual matching / original adaptation]
- Target: [duration, aspect ratio, delivery surface]
- Priority 1–3: [observable feature and acceptable tolerance]
- Inspection: [files, sampled timestamps/playback, audio inspected or unavailable]
- Constraints and gaps: [tools, access, budget, unknowns]

## Shot breakdown
Use the table in shot-analysis.md. Keep source timing separate from proposed output timing when durations differ. Do not compress actions without checking feasibility.

## Reference and continuity map
Identify each real attachment's role, priority, excluded influence, and unresolved conflicts. Record locks shared by all shots.

## Portable master brief
Describe the requested subject, environment, visual language, continuity rules, and overall sequence. Identify planned differences from the reference. Keep settings and postproduction tasks outside this text.

## Shot prompt
```text
Shot [ID], planned duration [duration].
Subject and setting: [concrete visible or user-supplied details].
Start state: [pose, location, props, framing].
Action: [ordered motion, direction, pace, interaction].
Camera: [one coherent camera instruction and framing].
End state: [pose, location, composition, continuity handoff].
Lighting and style: [observable desired characteristics].
Continuity: [locked identity, wardrobe, props, setting].
Audio: [only requested, supported, source-grounded content; otherwise separate plan].
```

Provide generator-specific syntax separately when verified. Remove unsupported fields instead of fabricating controls. Attach reference media as actual inputs; the prompt alone does not transmit them.

## Settings and assembly
Record model/host, verified controls, required attachments, planned shot order, cuts, titles, audio, and tasks assigned to editing. Distinguish proposed durations and estimates from measured facts.

## First test
Choose the hardest representative shot. State what to inspect, how to compare, acceptable results, and what to change first if it fails. Record whether the test is proposed or performed.
