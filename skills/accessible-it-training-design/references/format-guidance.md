# Format guidance

## Teams workshops and software demos

- Send accessible practice instructions and files ahead of time. Check that a participant account can open them. Invite private requests about access needs and explain participation options at the start.
- Show where participants can enable captions. Offer voice and written responses for discussion where either demonstrates the same objective. Put exercise instructions in an accessible file or message and allow time to move between the meeting and the practice app.
- State the exact command, where to find it, and what changes. Pause for practice. For required tasks, verify access in the app and version employees use. Verify keyboard routes before documenting shortcuts.

## Job aids, slides, workbooks, and PDFs

- Use built-in headings and lists, descriptive links, exact control labels, and concise action steps. Keep essential instructions as text. Add alt text for informative images and mark decorative images appropriately. Pair color cues with labels.
- Give PowerPoint slides unique titles and inspect object reading order. In Word, keep tables simple and identify header rows when tables are needed.
- Check the actual PDF export for structure, reading order, and navigation. For fillable forms, check accessible field names, instructions, tab order, and keyboard entry. Verify meaningful content with a screen reader where possible.

## Recorded demos and LMS activities

- Review timed captions for accuracy, product names, and meaningful sounds. Describe essential visual changes through narration or provide audio description as needed. Publish a transcript or copyable steps, prompts, and verification cues for reference.
- Test the published activity in learner view. Check focus order and visibility, keyboard operation, feedback, and any timer or drag interaction. Provide an equivalent accessible route for interactions where the gesture itself is incidental to the objective.

## Example learner test

Give a participant the published quick reference guide and a fictional practice file. Ask them to share the existing file with a teammate and confirm that the teammate can open it. Observe whether the participant finds the guide section, follows the file-sharing steps, and verifies access without coaching. Record any barrier and retest after revision.
