# Accessible IT Training Design

## Scope the task

Identify the employee's task, what success looks like, the app and platform, the training format, and how employees will receive it. Use the supplied asset and project constraints. Ask one question if a missing detail changes the design or finding materially. Otherwise proceed with a stated assumption. Ask about access preferences or barriers. Use fictional or approved practice data and keep existing approval boundaries.

Follow the employee's path through four actions: open the material, find or understand the instructions, complete the real task, and verify the result. Include transitions such as Teams to a practice file or a job aid to the work app. Keep the performance criterion consistent across participation and input methods. Where the target app has a barrier, document its effect and identify any verified route that achieves the same outcome.

## Design mode

1. Describe the task and its success condition. Identify likely access barriers before choosing formats or interactions.
2. Choose ways to receive instructions, participate, and practice that fit the audience and delivery setup. Build essential instructions into accessible text and select supports that address the identified barriers.
3. Specify what to build and what to test in the employee-facing version. Include a short learner test with a realistic scenario and observable result. Read the relevant sections of [Format guidance](references/format-guidance.md).

## Review mode

1. Identify the asset version, the view or export examined, and the checks you can perform. Inspect the actual published or exported version when available. A text extract can support wording findings. Inspect rendered layout, semantics, or interactive behavior separately when they matter.
2. For each material issue, give its location, evidence, learner impact, priority, and a concrete fix. Lead with blockers that prevent task completion. Preserve the author's wording, sequence, and intended outcome unless the request authorizes edits. Label suggested enhancements separately from observed barriers.
3. Return a brief test plan for the highest-impact workflow. Give the learner a scenario without coaching, ask them to use their usual setup and supports, and record whether they can find the instructions, complete the task, and verify the result. Note where they stopped or sought help, then retest after revision. Use representative employees, including assistive technology users when relevant and available.

## Report evidence

Use **Checked**, **Needs testing**, and **Not applicable** for individual checks. For checked items, report the method and result. Clearly label expected outcomes and draft fixes as proposals. Automated checkers cover part of the review. Report keyboard, screen-reader, platform, and learner testing only when it was performed. Avoid conformance claims based on a sample, a preview, or an automated scan. If requested to map findings to a standard, use the specified version and level and verify the applicable criteria against official guidance.

Keep the deliverable proportional. A review of one workflow can use a single finding and a short test. For a release review, include an ordered list of fixes and any remaining validation.
