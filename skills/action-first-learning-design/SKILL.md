# Action-First Learning Design

## Establish the target

- Use the supplied objective, segment, audience, delivery mode, and timebox. Translate a topic into one observable workplace action and identify the work product or decision that demonstrates it.
- Ask one focused question if the target action or a consequential constraint cannot be inferred. Otherwise state any material assumption and continue. Preserve approved objectives, timing, and evidence rules when revising existing materials.
- Use documented procedures and supplied policies as the answer-key authority. Mark any unresolved product behavior, access, or policy dependency for verification. Use fictional or sanitized materials and a practice environment when the exercise could affect work data or live systems.

## Design the practice

1. **Open with a workplace challenge.** Present the role, situation, relevant source material, and required response. Ask for a choice, attempt, or prediction before revealing the method. Give only the orientation needed to start. For novices, offer an optional cue or simplify the first task while preserving the target decision. When a full attempt requires access, permission, or a prerequisite, start with a safe decision or prediction about the same task.
2. **Respond to what learners did.** Identify likely responses and the cues learners may miss. Give feedback that explains what follows from each plausible choice and how to improve it. Demonstrate only the steps needed for the task. In self-paced training, place that feedback after the response and let learners proceed to the retry.
3. **Create an independent retry.** Change the case details while keeping the target action and difficulty comparable. Supply the full practice inputs. State what assistance is permitted, how to capture evidence, and what counts as success. Track assisted work separately if independence is required. Keep facilitator answers separate from learner materials.
4. **Close with reflection.** Ask one question that helps learners identify the cue, action, or verification step they will use on their next comparable work task.

Match the format to the work. Use hands-on practice for procedures, a short branching scenario for decisions with plausible consequences, or sorting and matching when classification or sequencing is the job skill. Start with the smallest format that preserves the decision and its consequences. Provide readable text and an equivalent response method that demonstrates the same skill. For live sessions, choose a response channel and evidence collection method the facilitator can manage in the time available.

## Deliver

- Give the job action, duration, materials, and any assumptions or prerequisites.
- Provide timed facilitator steps or the self-paced sequence, learner-facing instructions, and usable sample materials for both cases.
- Provide a facilitator key with expected evidence, feedback for common errors, the retry success criterion, and the closing reflection.
- When reviewing, identify the first meaningful learner action and where feedback, fresh practice, or transfer is missing. Propose the smallest revisions that fix those gaps.
- Check that timing adds up, the opener preserves the decision, the retry measures the same action, and the evidence is observable in the stated delivery mode. When the exercise uses a simulation, describe observed performance as practice evidence.

Kapp's *Action-First Learning* (ATD Press, 2025), especially the early attempt, ongoing action, and active reflection described in the [ATD sample chapter](https://assets.td.org/m/75165ec7e608c40f/original/112505_Action-First-Learning_Sample-Chapter.pdf).
