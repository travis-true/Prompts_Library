# Review standards

## Review categories

Every review must consider:

1. Purpose and learner need
2. Audience and prerequisites
3. Observable performance and learning objectives
4. Content scope and sequence
5. Instruction and demonstration
6. Learner practice
7. Feedback and debrief
8. Assessment and success evidence
9. Timing and breaks
10. Accessibility and inclusion
11. Facilitator usability
12. Materials, technology, and delivery readiness
13. Source authority and traceability
14. Higher-risk content controls when applicable

## Evidence-based rule

A finding must be grounded in one of these evidence types:

- Explicit outline text
- Missing required element
- Internal contradiction
- Timing calculation
- Objective-alignment gap
- Conflict with user-supplied standards
- Verified authoritative source

A preference without material impact should be labeled Enhancement, not a defect.

## Performance-first standard

A strong outline makes clear:

- What learners must do after training
- What evidence will demonstrate success
- Which content is required for that performance
- Where learners practice
- How learners receive feedback
- How success is checked

## Preserve-first standard

Preserve the outline's:

- Intended purpose
- Useful content
- Familiar structure
- Required terminology
- Valid timing and activities
- User-supplied constraints

Restructure only when evidence shows that sequence, timing, alignment, accessibility, or facilitation materially improves.

## Completeness is not quality

Do not approve an outline solely because it contains many sections. Determine whether the sections work together to produce the intended learner performance.
