# Authentic activity and feedback guidance

Use in **Design** when a workplace action genuinely needs practice. These are original design heuristics; do not treat them as a rigid formula.

## Practice activity anatomy

1. **Job context:** the learner's role, work setting, time pressures, conflicting demands, relevant conditions.
2. **Credible stimulus:** fictional email, project note, policy snippet, workbook, or interface representation; no real customer or employee records without authorization.
3. **Meaningful choice/action:** decide what to do next, transform a work product, locate the source, correct a mistake, use a tool, or escalate an access issue.
4. **Realistic constraints:** ambiguous information, incomplete input, competing priorities, reasonable mistakes.
5. **Feedback:** show the consequence and why it matters, not only `correct/incorrect`. Allow a second attempt and guidance where appropriate.
6. **Minimum needed support:** policies, checklists, job aids, examples, decision trees, or search tools should be available if available on the job.
7. **Observable evidence:** what artifact, decision, path, or completed task demonstrates performance. Define assisted practice and independent performance separately.
8. **Accessibility and platform:** keyboard/assistive-tech access; plain language; non-color cues; captions/transcripts if media is used; technical prerequisites unverified unless observed.

## Formats by need

- **Software task:** learner completes a workflow in a disposable environment; observe end state, safety, and source verification.
- **Judgment task:** learner makes a decision in a nuanced case and sees consequences; use plausible distractors, not trivia.
- **Collaboration task:** learner produces a usable handoff with ownership, deadlines, and unresolved questions.
- **Retrieval task:** learner finds the *correct* decision or source and explains confidence in its provenance.

## Avoid

- Giving a lengthy feature overview before learners attempt any realistic task.
- Artificial wrong answers or penalizing a valid alternative route.
- Expecting memorization of information employees can reliably look up during actual work.
- Claiming a fictional sample outcome demonstrates effectiveness.
- Overwriting existing approved assessment/scoring requirements to make the design more activity-first.

## Prototype test questions

Can a representative learner understand the work challenge? Do they make the intended decision? Are choices credible? Is feedback meaningful? Is the needed information accessible? Can the success criteria be observed? Are platform assumptions and assessment time feasible?
