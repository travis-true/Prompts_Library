# Verification and responsible use

## Claim contract
Record one claim ID and bounded statement per material assertion, asset IDs, source IDs and supporting location/paraphrase, official URL/title/publisher, published/updated date when present, access date, scope, dependencies, documentation status, tenant-test status/evidence, owner and refresh/expiration trigger. Never invent a source date. Documentation checked is not tenant tested. General prompting advice cannot verify file upload, conversation access, app-specific behavior or licensing for every employee.

Classify current documentation, environment-dependent, license-dependent, preview, announced, retired, Not verified, or supplied requirement. Research changing product claims against official Microsoft sources; verify conflicting local policy with its owner. If browsing is unavailable, preserve supported supplied evidence and mark unresolved claims Not verified; never fabricate verification. Claims past refresh date or changed dependencies must be rechecked before release.

## Grounding and human review
Declare exactly which experience and approved inputs are used. Provide complete pasteable fictional inputs as a fallback where appropriate; do not assert that all products accept the same attachments. A text alternative preserves only the documented task, not untested feature equivalence. Ask for missing facts rather than inventing owners, dates or commitments. Verify draft facts against sources; an automated critique is assistance, not independent truth verification. Do not turn de-identification instructions into a guarantee: review sanitized examples before any sharing.

## Evidence
Record actual method/date/reviewer/version and evidence location for performed checks; keep unperformed checks Not checked. Product guidance alone does not authorize data use or publication. Stop factual release of material unresolved or expired claims; provide a supported narrower draft or a prepared escalation.
