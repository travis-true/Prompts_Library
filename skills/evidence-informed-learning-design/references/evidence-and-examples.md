# Evidence and examples

## Sources

- Mirjam Neelen and Paul A. Kirschner, *Evidence-Informed Learning Design: Creating Training to Improve Performance*, Kogan Page, 2020, ISBN 9781789661415. The [publisher’s chapter outline](https://cdn.koganpage.com/media/public/document/evidence-informed-learning-design_01_-neelen_9781789661415_toc.pdf) maps evidence appraisal to chapters 1 and 4, complex skills to chapter 7, instruction and feedback to chapter 9, worked examples and practice strategies to chapter 10, and self-regulation to chapters 11–12. Use it to locate topics.
- The [public chapter 1 excerpt](https://www.perlego.com/book/1589963/evidenceinformed-learning-design-creating-training-to-improve-performance-pdf) explains how to combine research, design expertise, and evidence from the workplace. Treat the excerpt and outline as partial access to the book.
- Paul A. Kirschner, Mirjam Neelen, and Tim Surma, [Maximizing the Effectiveness of Workplace Learning](https://www.aft.org/ae/spring2024/kirschner_neelen_surma), 2024. Use for applied guidance on modeling, guided practice, feedback, independent practice, and review.
- Jeroen J. G. van Merriënboer and Paul A. Kirschner, [Ten Steps to Complex Learning](https://research.ou.nl/files/941352/Ten%20Steps%20to%20Complex%20Learning%20-%20Sage%2021st%20Century.pdf). Use for whole-task design, simple-to-complex progression, and scaffolding.

Trace new research claims to their underlying source where available. Check who and what was studied, the comparison, what outcome was measured, and how closely the conditions match the workplace. Cite sources for the instructional claim they support. Label example designs, local observations, and proposed pilot measures as such.

## Illustrative applications

**OneNote for recurring meeting notes.** Model how to organize a set of fictional notes and retrieve a previous decision. Explain the naming choices. For guided practice, supply a partially organized notebook and offer feedback, then ask learners to retry. For independent practice, use a new recurring meeting and remove the scaffolds specified in the assessment plan. Observe the agreed actions and the resulting notebook or retrieval. If an action cannot be observed, record missing evidence. A single prior retrieval attempt can inform a scenario and a pilot question.

**Copilot for meeting follow-up.** Use fictional source notes and a prepared recap that assigns an unsupported action owner. Model checking each action against the notes. During practice, give targeted feedback on an unsupported owner and let learners correct the draft. In the independent check, give a new recap and judge whether each action and owner is supported, with missing owners marked for confirmation. A prepared recap can exercise verification when access to a live Copilot feature is unconfirmed. If the objective requires live tool use, treat access as a prerequisite and explain what the simulation can assess.

**M365 sharing decisions.** After learners have practiced individual options, send a new case that asks them to choose an option and explain why before revealing feedback. Mix cases with similar surface details and different recipient or access needs when choosing between options is the target. Base cases on the organization’s verified sharing options.
