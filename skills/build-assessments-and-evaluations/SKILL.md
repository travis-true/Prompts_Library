# Assessment and Evaluation Builder

Build evidence that supports a defined decision. Measure the intended knowledge, skill, behavior, experience, or operational result without overstating what the evidence proves.

## Governing rules

- Begin with the decision the evidence must support.
- Distinguish assessment, evaluation, measurement, feedback, and research.
- Align each measure to an observable outcome and appropriate evidence.
- Use only supplied or authoritative sources to determine correct answers, required performance, standards, and scoring criteria.
- Never invent answer keys, standards, cut scores, validation, reliability, results, participant data, approvals, or effectiveness claims.
- Prefer direct performance evidence when the outcome is performance.
- Do not use satisfaction, attendance, confidence, completion, or self-report as a substitute for demonstrated competence.
- Use the minimum evidence necessary for the decision and risk.
- Apply accessibility, UDL, privacy, classification, data minimization, fairness, and permitted-use requirements throughout.
- Separate formative feedback from summative judgment.
- State uncertainty, limitations, alternative explanations, and missing evidence.
- Require accountable human review for high-stakes or consequential uses.

## Operating modes

Select one:

1. **Design:** Define questions, evidence, methods, blueprint, scoring, administration, analysis, and governance.
2. **Build:** Create the selected assessment or evaluation instruments and answer/scoring materials.
3. **Revise:** Improve existing instruments while preserving authorized objectives, content, or comparability.
4. **Review:** Audit alignment, item quality, scoring, accessibility, fairness, data handling, and interpretive claims.
5. **Analyze:** Evaluate supplied response or performance data using an approved analysis plan.

If the requested platform or file type cannot be created, provide an implementation-ready specification and do not imply that a working instrument was produced.

## Workflow

### 1. Establish the evidence contract

Determine:

- decision, decision owner, and intended action;
- business, performance, learning, transfer, or program outcome;
- audience, roles, experience, language, access, and accommodations;
- assessment or evaluation purpose and stakes;
- authoritative sources and required standards;
- administration setting, modality, timing, tools, and permitted supports;
- sample or participant population;
- required platform, format, scoring, reporting, and retention;
- privacy, classification, consent, security, accessibility, and approval requirements;
- consequences of false positive, false negative, or misleading interpretation.

Ask grouped clarifying questions only when missing answers could materially change the design. Target roughly 95% confidence when questions are permitted. If immediate work is required, continue with explicit assumptions and limits.

### 2. Classify the measurement need

Read [references/measurement-types.md](references/measurement-types.md). Select the smallest defensible combination:

- diagnostic;
- formative;
- summative knowledge;
- practical or performance;
- scenario-based judgment;
- observation;
- confidence or self-efficacy;
- reaction or experience;
- transfer or behavior;
- operational result;
- implementation or process evaluation.

Do not add a pre-assessment, post-assessment, survey, or quiz by convention. Each must support a named decision.

### 3. Define the intended interpretation

State:

> If **[evidence]** meets **[criteria]** under **[conditions]**, the decision owner may conclude **[bounded interpretation]** and take **[action]**.

Also state what the evidence cannot establish.

Examples:

- A knowledge quiz may support a conclusion about recall or recognition, not independent task performance.
- A practical assessment may support competence only for the sampled tasks, conditions, and scoring standard.
- A reaction survey may support delivery improvement, not learning effectiveness.
- A pre/post change may show association with the intervention, not causation without an appropriate design.

### 4. Map outcomes to evidence

For each outcome, identify:

- observable behavior, decision, product, or response;
- conditions, tools, resources, and constraints;
- performance standard or quality criteria;
- evidence type;
- method and timing;
- scoring or interpretation;
- risk and consequence;
- owner and reviewer.

Read [references/alignment-and-blueprints.md](references/alignment-and-blueprints.md). Remove measures that do not map to an outcome or decision.

### 5. Create the blueprint

Define:

- content or performance domains;
- objective or competency coverage;
- cognitive or performance demand;
- method and item/task type;
- number and distribution of items or observations;
- time and permitted supports;
- weighting and critical criteria;
- scoring and feedback;
- parallel or alternate forms where needed;
- accessibility and administration requirements.

Use stable IDs for objectives, items, tasks, criteria, instruments, and decisions.

### 6. Select the evidence method

Choose the method that most directly and feasibly measures the intended outcome:

- selected-response items for efficient sampling of knowledge or decisions;
- constructed response for explanation, reasoning, or production;
- scenario for applied judgment;
- simulation or work sample for realistic performance;
- observation for behavior under defined conditions;
- portfolio or artifact review for cumulative evidence;
- survey or interview for perception, experience, barriers, or self-report;
- operational data for workplace outcomes and process results.

Use multiple methods only when each contributes unique evidence or reduces material risk.

### 7. Develop items, tasks, and rubrics

Read [references/item-and-rubric-standards.md](references/item-and-rubric-standards.md).

For each item or task:

- link to one or more objectives and sources;
- identify the evidence sought;
- match difficulty and authenticity to the intended use;
- write clear, accessible instructions;
- remove irrelevant complexity and cultural or linguistic barriers;
- define correct, acceptable, partially acceptable, and unacceptable performance;
- include rationale and feedback when appropriate;
- flag critical errors, automatic-stop conditions, or escalation;
- create scoring notes and examples without leaking controlled answers to learners.

Do not make items difficult through ambiguity, trivia, trick wording, excessive reading, or unsupported distractors.

### 8. Set scoring and decision rules

Use criterion-referenced rules when performance standards exist. Determine:

- score type and calculation;
- critical versus compensable criteria;
- weights and rationale;
- pass, proficiency, or decision rule;
- handling of partial credit;
- missing, invalid, or incomplete evidence;
- retake, remediation, reassessment, and appeal;
- reviewer calibration and adjudication;
- confidence and limitations.

Never default to 70%, 80%, or another conventional threshold without a defensible basis. For high-risk work, failure on a critical safety, policy, privacy, or control criterion may override the total score.

### 9. Design administration

Specify:

- who administers, observes, scores, supports, and approves;
- standard instructions and conditions;
- time, attempts, tools, references, collaboration, and accommodations;
- identity and evidence requirements where justified;
- technical requirements and fallback;
- data collection, storage, access, retention, and deletion;
- security, item exposure, versioning, and incident handling;
- learner notice, feedback, results, and support.

Provide equivalent access without changing the construct being measured. If an accommodation changes the construct, document and obtain the required decision.

### 10. Design evaluation and analysis

Read [references/evaluation-and-analysis.md](references/evaluation-and-analysis.md). Define:

- evaluation questions;
- indicators and data sources;
- baseline or comparison where appropriate;
- timing and sample;
- collection owner and analysis owner;
- planned calculations and qualitative method;
- disaggregation and fairness checks where permitted;
- missing-data and response-bias handling;
- decision thresholds;
- limitations and alternative explanations;
- reporting audience and action.

Do not collect data merely because a platform makes it easy.

### 11. Pilot and quality-check

When risk or scale justifies it:

- conduct expert content review;
- test instructions, timing, usability, accessibility, and technology;
- verify keys, calculations, branching, feedback, and score reporting;
- pilot with representative users;
- examine item or task performance;
- calibrate raters and compare scoring;
- review subgroup effects where lawful and appropriate;
- revise and version affected materials.

Do not call an instrument validated because it was reviewed, piloted once, or produced successfully.

### 12. Produce and hand off

Use [references/output-templates.md](references/output-templates.md). Deliver only applicable components:

- evidence contract;
- alignment map and blueprint;
- learner-facing instrument;
- answer key or model response;
- rubric or observation guide;
- administration instructions;
- scoring and decision rules;
- feedback and remediation;
- evaluation and analysis plan;
- data dictionary and reporting specification;
- QA, version, approval, and maintenance record.

Separate controlled scoring materials from learner-facing materials when necessary.

### 13. Analyze and report

When valid data is supplied:

- follow the approved analysis plan;
- preserve raw data and transformation traceability;
- verify calculations and sample definitions;
- distinguish observed results from interpretation;
- report uncertainty, missingness, response rate, bias, and limitations;
- avoid causal claims unsupported by the design;
- connect findings to the named decision;
- identify the smallest justified action.

Do not expose personally identifiable, sensitive, or restricted data beyond the permitted audience and purpose.

### 14. Maintain

Set review triggers for changes to:

- objectives, competency, policy, process, system, or source;
- audience, language, accessibility, modality, or platform;
- scoring or decision consequences;
- item exposure or security;
- recurring errors or ambiguous performance;
- evidence of bias, poor reliability, weak discrimination, or invalid interpretation;
- regulations, privacy, retention, or reporting.

Retire obsolete forms and preserve comparability notes when instruments change.

## Quality gate

Before release, verify:

- decision and bounded interpretation are explicit;
- outcomes, evidence, methods, items, scoring, and action align;
- correct answers and criteria are source-grounded;
- difficulty comes from the target performance rather than bad wording;
- accessibility and equivalent administration are supported;
- scoring is reproducible and critical criteria are protected;
- privacy, classification, security, retention, and access are controlled;
- analysis and reporting claims do not exceed the design;
- high-stakes use has adequate review, pilot evidence, human authority, and appeal or remediation where required;
- all unperformed checks are labeled `NOT VERIFIED`.
