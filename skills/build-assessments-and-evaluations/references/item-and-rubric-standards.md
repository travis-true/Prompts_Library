# Item, task, and rubric standards

## Selected-response items

- Write a clear question or incomplete statement with one intended interpretation.
- Include all shared wording in the stem.
- Avoid unnecessary negatives; emphasize a necessary negative.
- Keep options parallel in grammar, length, specificity, and plausibility.
- Use one best answer unless multiple-response behavior is explicit.
- Avoid clues from wording, absolutes, option length, patterns, or overlap.
- Use plausible distractors based on real misconceptions or errors.
- Avoid “all of the above” and “none of the above” unless strongly justified.
- Do not test trivia unrelated to the outcome.
- Supply the key, rationale, source, objective, demand, and feedback.

## True/false

Use sparingly for clear, consequential propositions. Avoid compound claims, qualifiers that give away the answer, and statements whose truth changes by context.

## Constructed response

- State the required product, scope, conditions, and criteria.
- Provide enough context to answer without guessing unstated facts.
- Define acceptable evidence, variants, and partial credit.
- Use a rubric or scoring guide for consequential decisions.

## Scenarios

- Make the setting realistic and relevant to the role.
- Include only information the performer would reasonably have.
- Measure the target judgment rather than reading endurance.
- Preserve ambiguity only when managing ambiguity is part of the outcome.
- Require reasoning or evidence when the decision process matters.
- Include consequences, exceptions, escalation, and incomplete information when authentic.

## Performance tasks

- Replicate critical conditions, tools, constraints, and outputs.
- Permit references or tools that are available in real work.
- Observe both result and critical process where necessary.
- Define start state, task, time, evidence, success, critical errors, and stop conditions.
- Use fictional, masked, synthetic, or explicitly permitted data.
- Provide equivalent access without removing the target performance.

## Rubrics

Use criteria that are:

- distinct;
- observable;
- relevant to the outcome;
- source-grounded;
- feasible to score consistently.

For each level:

- describe performance, not personality;
- use observable anchors;
- distinguish adjacent levels meaningfully;
- avoid vague labels such as “good” without evidence;
- identify critical failures and non-compensable criteria.

Use analytic rubrics when separate criteria support feedback or decisions. Use holistic judgments only when the overall performance is the construct and raters can be calibrated.

## Observation checklists

Record:

- opportunity to observe;
- performed, not performed, not applicable, or not observed;
- evidence or note;
- criticality;
- observer;
- date, context, and version.

Do not score “not observed” as failure without a defined rule.

## Surveys

- Measure one construct per item.
- Use neutral, plain, specific wording.
- Provide balanced response options and labeled scales.
- Define the time frame and referent.
- Avoid double-barreled, leading, loaded, or mandatory open-text questions.
- Include “not applicable” or “not enough opportunity” when valid.
- Do not collect identity or sensitive detail without a decision need.
