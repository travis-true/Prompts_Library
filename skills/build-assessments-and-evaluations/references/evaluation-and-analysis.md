# Evaluation and analysis

## Evaluation question types

- Was the intervention accessible, usable, and delivered as designed?
- Did participants acquire the intended knowledge or skill?
- Can participants perform under defined conditions?
- Is the behavior used at work when opportunity exists?
- What barriers, supports, and contextual factors affect transfer?
- Did operational indicators change?
- Should the intervention continue, scale, change, pause, or retire?

## Data principles

- Collect the minimum data needed.
- Define each field, source, unit, period, population, and calculation.
- Separate identifiable operational needs from optional anonymous feedback.
- Set access, retention, deletion, and reporting rules before collection.
- Do not promise anonymity when responses or metadata can identify people.
- Avoid collecting confidential work content when task completion or rubric results suffice.

## Quantitative analysis

As applicable:

- response, completion, and missing-data rates;
- score distribution and criterion performance;
- pre/post change with paired-sample rules;
- item difficulty and discrimination;
- inter-rater agreement;
- subgroup and accessibility patterns where lawful and sufficiently supported;
- operational trends and comparison periods;
- uncertainty, practical significance, and sensitivity.

Verify denominators and avoid percentages based on misleadingly small or selected samples.

## Qualitative analysis

- Define coding questions and units.
- Preserve context and contradictory evidence.
- Separate recurring themes from isolated comments.
- Avoid counting sentiment as performance.
- Remove or protect identifiers.
- Use representative excerpts sparingly and within permission.

## Causal restraint

Training is one possible contributor among:

- opportunity to perform;
- manager reinforcement;
- process or policy change;
- access and tool availability;
- workload and staffing;
- incentives;
- concurrent communications or interventions;
- participant selection;
- time and external events.

Use causal language only when the design and evidence justify it. Otherwise state association, contribution, or observed change.

## Reporting

Lead with:

- decision question;
- evidence;
- finding;
- limitation;
- recommended action.

Distinguish:

- observed result;
- interpretation;
- inference;
- recommendation;
- unresolved question.
