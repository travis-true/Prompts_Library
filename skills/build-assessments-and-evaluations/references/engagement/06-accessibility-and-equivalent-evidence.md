# Accessibility and equivalent evidence

## Design requirements

- Provide instructions in text and aloud when delivered live.
- Use logical headings and plain language.
- Provide processing time.
- Avoid requiring cameras unless the objective requires visual performance.
- Avoid relying on color, sound, gesture, position, dragging, handwriting, or speech alone.
- Support assistive technology.

## Equivalent evidence rule

When response mode is not the objective, offer alternatives that measure the same performance standard.

Examples:

- Verbal response or written response
- Drag-and-drop sort or numbered categorization
- Live role play or written scenario response
- Shared whiteboard or individual worksheet
- Mouse-based task or keyboard-accessible path

## Accommodation guidance

State:

- What can vary
- What must remain constant
- What evidence is required
- How extra time or alternate format affects administration

Do not lower the success criteria unless an authorized accommodation decision explicitly changes the standard.

## Verification scope
Report accessibility-design, actual rendered materials, selected platform and fallback tests separately. Written alternatives are planned provisions, not proof of tested access. Label actual checks Not checked until performed. Protect the assessed construct when adapting response mode.
