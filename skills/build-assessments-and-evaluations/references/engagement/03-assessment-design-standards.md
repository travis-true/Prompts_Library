# Assessment design standards

## Assessment by objective

Match evidence to the objective:

- Identify or recognize: selection, labeling, categorization
- Explain: concise explanation using criteria
- Decide: scenario choice with rationale
- Perform procedure: observed task or checklist
- Create: artifact evaluated with rubric
- Troubleshoot: diagnosis and corrective action
- Evaluate: judgment using standards
- Communicate: produced message or demonstration assessed against criteria

## Stakes

### Low-stakes practice
Purpose: feedback and learning. Flexible administration, retries, hints, and coaching are expected.

### Proficiency check
Purpose: determine whether a learner meets a defined performance expectation. Requires clear criteria, consistent scoring, and remediation.

### High-stakes qualification
Purpose: authorization, certification, compliance, eligibility, or consequential decisions. Requires authoritative standards, validated criteria, controlled administration, qualified review, security, and documented governance.

## Item quality

- Test the objective, not trivia.
- Avoid clues and trick wording.
- Keep distractors plausible and tied to common misconceptions.
- Do not use “all of the above” or “none of the above” without a strong reason.
- Avoid unnecessary negative phrasing.
- Give enough information to answer without irrelevant reading burden.

## Passing standards

Do not invent thresholds. When none are supplied, provide descriptive proficiency levels and mark the cut score as `[Decision required]`.

## Consequence-first clarification
When stakes are unknown and result use suggests certification, authorization, eligibility, qualification or employment consequences, ask what decision the results support before classifying stakes or producing consequential scoring. Preserve unresolved stakes and block consequential decisions. Do not silently default to low stakes. A practice-only clarification must explicitly exclude consequence use; record that answer. An intentional high-stakes request needs authoritative standards and qualified review, not merely a harder quiz.

## Criterion feedback versus a decision
Without an authorized passing standard, report descriptive criterion evidence only, with no aggregate passing inference. A proposed formative target is a recommendation, not an official proficiency threshold. Keep all tables, keys, rubrics and metadata consistent. A multiple-choice rationale may support diagnosis but cannot alone establish production/operation performance.
