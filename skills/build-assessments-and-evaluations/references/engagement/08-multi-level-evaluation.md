# Multi-level evaluation

## Level 1: Learner reaction
Measure usefulness, relevance, confidence, accessibility, and delivery quality. Do not treat satisfaction as proof of learning.

## Level 2: Knowledge or skill acquisition
Use objective-aligned knowledge checks, practical tasks, demonstrations, artifacts, or simulations.

## Level 3: On-the-job application
Use follow-up self-report, manager observation, work-sample review, task evidence, or workflow adoption indicators. Define the observation window but do not invent a timeframe when none is supplied.

## Level 4: Observable performance outcomes
Use operational measures that plausibly connect to the trained performance. Examples may include error rate, cycle time, quality, rework, adoption, or service outcomes when appropriate and authorized.

## Causality discipline

Training may contribute to outcomes, but do not claim causation without an appropriate evaluation design. Record other factors, baseline data, comparison methods, and evidence limitations.

## Evaluation record

For each level, define:

- Question
- Indicator
- Data source
- Collection owner
- Timing or trigger
- Analysis method
- Interpretation limits
- Action based on results

## Proportional measures
Select only levels that serve a concrete decision with an owner and feasible data. Explicitly mark unselected levels as deferred/not applicable with rationale; no automatic four-level evaluation for a single poll. Proposed owner roles/timing are not accepted assignments. Distinguish self-report, reviewed work samples and operational outcomes; limit causal interpretation and unnecessary collection.
