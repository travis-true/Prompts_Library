# Multimedia principles in software training

| Principle | Design decision |
|---|---|
| Multimedia (ch. 4) | Pair words with visuals when seeing the interface, a process, or a result helps the learner act. Show the starting state and the result when the difference matters. |
| Contiguity (ch. 5) | Put short instructions by the corresponding screenshot detail. In a recording, say the control name as the cue appears and hold the frame long enough to locate it. |
| Signaling (ch. 6) | Use a specific cue for the current control or decision. For nearby controls with similar names, retain enough surrounding context to identify the right one. |
| Modality and redundancy (chs. 7–8) | In a narrated demo with moving visuals, use short designed overlays for exact labels, values, or instructions learners need to refer back to. Treat captions and accessible written procedures as part of access. Choose text-led instruction for print, silent use, or steps that need frequent reference. |
| Coherence (ch. 9) | Remove elements that compete with the task, such as irrelevant inbox activity, extra transitions, or background music that competes with instructions. Keep context that helps learners orient themselves or understand the result. |
| Segmenting and pretraining (ch. 13) | Give learners pacing or replay control at meaningful stages. Explain an unfamiliar term when it first becomes necessary for a decision. For live delivery, pause at a completed action or decision and let participants catch up. |

## Apply by format

- **Camtasia or recorded demo.** Review the narration, cue timing, interface movement, and caption placement together. Pair each instruction with the corresponding visual state and show how the learner can tell the action worked.
- **Rise 360 or self-paced e-learning.** Preview at the intended viewport. Keep the instruction and referenced visual close on reflow. Make navigation, restart, or replay work at points learners may need to revisit.
- **Teams slides and live demos.** Design for the audience's shared-screen view. Show only the cues people need to follow the current action, and give participants time to perform it. Ensure the distributed resource still carries the instructions learners need on their own.
- **Screenshot job aids.** Use a numbered sequence and match each label to a visible control. Keep action and expected result in readable text. Check print and screen legibility, and ensure meaningful screenshot content is conveyed through the adjacent instructions or a text equivalent.
