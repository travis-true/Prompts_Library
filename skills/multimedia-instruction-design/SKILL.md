# Multimedia Instruction Design

## Review or design the material

1. Identify the workplace task, audience experience, app or platform, delivery format, and conditions of use. Preserve approved objectives, procedures, terminology, and accessibility requirements. Confirm which pages, slides, frames, captions, or source files you can inspect. If you have a script or transcript alone, review the wording and flag visual timing for inspection in the recording. Ask a focused question only if a missing detail prevents a useful review.
2. Follow the task in the order a learner would perform it. Mark the controls, decisions, prerequisites, and visible success or error states. Locate the places where a learner must search the screen, remember an earlier instruction, read a dense overlay while following a moving interface, or infer what changed.
3. Select the principles that address those issues. Read [principles and examples](references/principles-and-examples.md) for guidance by format, research boundaries, or citation details. Check that each cue points to the intended control, each label sits near its referent, and each spoken instruction aligns with the relevant action. Retain context needed to distinguish similar controls. Break complex flows at meaningful actions or decisions and introduce only the prerequisites learners need to understand them.
4. Check the revised experience in the intended format. Preserve captions and accessible equivalents, including meaningful interface actions conveyed visually. Check that overlays and captions leave required controls visible. Pair color with a label, outline, or other cue. For print or silent use, make essential instructions and results available in readable text. Flag device, playback, app version, or tenant-dependent details that need direct verification.
5. Prioritize edits by their likely consequence for performing the task. Give a verification step that someone can carry out, such as watching with audio muted, checking a rendered screenshot at its expected size, or asking a representative learner to locate the control and confirm the result. Distinguish observations from proposed edits and unverified details. Apply edits when requested and report the review evidence you actually examined.

## Deliverable

For a review, return a concise prioritized table with location (screen, slide, page, step, or timestamp), observed issue and learner impact, proposed edit, and verification. Merge repeated findings only when the same edit resolves them and identify the affected locations. For a new design or redesign, return a screen-by-screen storyboard with the learner action, visual and cue, narration or written instruction as appropriate, and the expected result. If job practice, feedback, or assessment design is the main request, use the evidence-informed learning design workflow.
