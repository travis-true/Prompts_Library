# Course design standards

## Performance-first model

Build from the desired learner performance backward:

1. Define what the learner must do after training.
2. Identify the evidence that would demonstrate success.
3. Write measurable objectives.
4. Select only the content needed to perform.
5. Demonstrate or model the performance.
6. Provide supported practice.
7. Provide feedback.
8. Check independent success.

## Course structure

A live course should normally include:

- Orientation and relevance
- Objectives and success criteria
- Activation of prior knowledge
- Short instruction or explanation
- Demonstration or worked example
- Guided practice
- Feedback or debrief
- Independent or less-supported practice
- Assessment or success check
- Transfer planning and close

Not every module needs every element, but every objective needs instruction, practice, and evidence.

## Scope control

Classify content as:

- Core: Required to meet the objectives
- Supporting: Helps understanding but is not directly assessed
- Optional: Useful if time remains
- Advanced: For experienced learners or follow-up
- Verify before delivery: Environment-dependent, changing, or unresolved
- Out of scope: Does not support the stated performance

## Scenario guidance

Use one coherent fictional or approved scenario when it reduces cognitive load and supports skill transfer. The scenario must:

- Match the audience and topic
- Avoid real confidential or personal data
- Contain enough detail to support all practices
- Remain consistent across demonstrations and exercises
- Avoid unnecessary complexity

## Facilitator usability

Facilitator guidance must be scannable during delivery. For each module, include:

- Purpose
- Timing
- Facilitator action
- Learner action
- Materials
- Exact activity or assessment prompt when useful
- Expected evidence
- Debrief or transition
- Fallback

## Neutral style

- Use plain language.
- Use accessible heading order.
- Avoid proprietary branding.
- Use labels and text in addition to color.
- Keep tables simple.
- Label placeholders in square brackets.

## Transfer beyond the session
Include one authentic application task, a suggested follow-up interval, a support owner or role to confirm, and observable evidence of use. Distinguish attendance, in-session success, later application and business outcomes; do not claim that completing training caused an outcome without supporting evidence.
