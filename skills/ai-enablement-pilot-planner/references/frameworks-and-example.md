# Frameworks and example

## Torrance's frameworks

Megan Torrance's *The AI Implementation Guide for L&D* (ATD Press, 2026, ISBN 9781963392296) provides the three lenses below. Use them to guide planning conversations and tailor the depth to the pilot.

- **AI Impact Zones.** Identify whether AI assists the training team, changes the learner's experience, or changes how people do their jobs. One initiative can span several zones. Choose a primary outcome for the task being tested.
- **AI Implementation Canvas.** Torrance groups 14 dimensions into four areas. Ask what workplace result warrants the effort (strategic alignment), which people, tools, inputs, and review steps make the workflow possible (technology and experience), how to test it and decide whether to expand (design and implementation), and what practice or support employees need (human-centered adoption).
- **W.I.S.E. A.T. A.I.** Translate the responsible-use principles into checks suited to the task. For example, choose an appropriate use, make practice accessible, verify that participants can use the inputs, consider who may be excluded or misrepresented, assign accountability for the result, and follow applicable disclosure rules.

The planning prompts and worked example below are adaptations for workplace enablement. Sources are the [ATD sample](https://assets.td.org/m/1560adabfce239aa/original/112609_The-AI-Implementation-Guide-for-L-D_SampleChapter.pdf) and the [author's companion resources](https://www.torrancelearning.com/ai-implementation-canvas/).

## Worked example — Copilot meeting follow-up

**Workflow and outcome.** A recurring project meeting needs a recap with verified decisions, actions, owners, and deadlines. The meeting coordinator prepares and verifies it against the source notes before sharing. Record missing owners or deadlines as open questions.

**Readiness.** Begin with comparable fictional notes. Before using real notes, confirm with the relevant owners that the selected app, participants' access, and handling of meeting content are authorized. Provide the practice materials and checklist in an accessible format.

**Pilot and comparison.** Ask a small group of coordinators to draft one recap using their current process and one with AI assistance. Use two comparable fictional cases, vary the order, and apply the same checklist. If the workflow is cleared for real use, check for repeat use after two weeks among coordinators who had another meeting.

**Measures and decision.** Capture minutes from opening the notes to a verified draft, factual omissions and invented details requiring correction, and the number of eligible coordinators who reuse the method. Propose expanding only if the recaps meet the same quality bar, the total effort drops by an amount the owner considers meaningful, and the team can repeat the workflow with approved inputs. Revise and retest when the task or support creates avoidable friction. Pause if an unresolved data, access, or review problem prevents safe use.

**Owners and next action.** The workflow owner confirms the quality bar and the expansion decision. The enablement specialist prepares the examples and measures. The tool or data owner confirms access and input handling as applicable.
