# AI Enablement Pilot Planner

Use the request and existing discovery or action-mapping evidence to plan a test of one workplace task. For source attribution, framework prompts, and a worked Copilot example, read [Frameworks and example](references/frameworks-and-example.md).

## 1. Define the workflow

- Identify the people doing the work, the trigger, current process, intended AI contribution, and the output or behavior that counts as success. Identify the workflow owner and the person who will decide what happens after the pilot. Record unknown owners as roles to confirm.
- Use the relevant AI impact zone to choose the outcome. For training production, assess the approved asset and total production effort. For learner practice, assess performance on a task with a new example. For workplace adoption, assess the quality and effort of completing the actual work.
- Reuse agreed requirements, baseline evidence, and approvals. Ask one focused question if an unknown would materially change the design or recommendation. Otherwise, draft the plan with explicit assumptions and open decisions.

## 2. Check readiness

- Use the AI Implementation Canvas to check business value, readiness and ownership, how the tool fits the workflow, and how participants will learn and receive support. Identify access, supported tool or app, license or entitlement, approved inputs, and who can verify each dependency. Verify changing product claims against current official documentation when needed. Treat tenant configuration and actual participant access as locally verifiable.
- Use W.I.S.E. A.T. A.I. to translate relevant risks into testable behaviors. Specify who checks the output against the source, corrects or rejects it, and approves release. Consider accessibility, people with different access or experience, and when disclosure is required by applicable policy.
- Confirm relevant permission to use the tool and inputs, especially for sensitive material. If approval, access, or oversight remains unresolved, identify the owner and the next step. Where appropriate, propose a dry run with fictional data in an approved environment. Label pending checks and keep planning separate from pilot execution.

## 3. Design a fair, small test

- Propose a cohort, duration, comparison tasks, and review cadence suited to the workflow. Compare the existing and AI-assisted processes using comparable inputs, the same quality rubric, and similar task conditions. If participants do both, use different but comparable cases and vary order where feasible. If no baseline exists, specify how to collect it.
- Measure time from task start to a verified, usable result, including review and rework. Define what counts as a substantive correction and what makes an output unusable. Record unsuccessful attempts and why they failed. If measuring repeat use, define the time window and count only people who had an opportunity to repeat the task. Name who captures each measure and where.
- Propose advance criteria for expanding, revising and retesting, or pausing. Ask the decision owner to agree on the quality floor, practical improvement, and any stop condition. Mark suggested thresholds as proposals. When reviewing results, report sample size, incomplete observations, and limits on what the comparison supports.

## 4. Return a one-page plan

Use short sections for **Workflow and outcome**, **Readiness and open decisions**, **Pilot and comparison**, **Measures and decision rules**, and **Owners and next action**. Give a readiness recommendation supported by available evidence. If evidence is missing, show the next step needed to resolve it. When handing off to training design or evaluation, carry forward the observable task, quality checks, known barriers, and evidence to collect.
