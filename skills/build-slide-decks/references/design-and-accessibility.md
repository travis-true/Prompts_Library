# Design system and accessibility

## Visual system

Define and apply:

- slide size and aspect ratio;
- margins and grid;
- title, subtitle, body, callout, caption, and source hierarchy;
- palette roles rather than arbitrary colors;
- image, icon, illustration, chart, table, diagram, and screenshot treatments;
- section and closing behavior;
- footer, version, confidentiality, and page-number rules.

Keep the system coherent while varying layouts to match the content.

## Typography

Match the supplied template when one governs. Without a template, use at least:

- 50 pt deck title;
- 35 pt slide title;
- 24 pt subheading or callout heading;
- 16 pt body text.

Shorten copy or change the layout before reducing size. Prevent unexpected wrapping, widows, or clipped text. Use familiar typefaces with distinguishable weights.

## Layout

- Use balanced margins and clear alignment.
- Establish one obvious entry point and reading path.
- Keep related items close and distinct items separated.
- Avoid both crowding and unearned empty space.
- Prefer one strong composition over a collection of interface-like cards.
- Keep decorative elements subordinate to the content.
- Use whitespace to clarify hierarchy, not to hide missing content.

## Color and contrast

- Meet applicable contrast requirements.
- Do not communicate meaning by color alone.
- Test text over images and transparent overlays.
- Use a restrained palette with clear functional roles.
- Preserve brand rules without sacrificing readability.

## Visual assets

- Use relevant, high-resolution assets with appropriate rights and provenance.
- Choose aspect ratio, subject placement, crop, and visual weight for the intended frame.
- Avoid distorted, blurry, generic, culturally narrow, or misleading visuals.
- Use consistent visual language without repeating the same image by default.
- Provide meaningful alt text or mark decorative assets appropriately.

## Charts, tables, and diagrams

- Select a chart that matches the numeric relationship.
- Show units, periods, definitions, sample sizes, and limitations when material.
- Avoid unnecessary 3D, effects, dual axes, or precision.
- Highlight the evidence supporting the slide claim.
- Use tables for exact values and comparisons; avoid tiny text.
- Use diagrams only when relationships, topology, sequence, or hierarchy become clearer.
- Keep labels concise and connector semantics consistent.

## Screenshots and demonstrations

- Use fictional, masked, synthetic, or explicitly permitted data.
- Match the correct product, version, device, state, and workflow.
- Limit callouts to the cues needed for the current action.
- Provide alt text and describe important visual changes during delivery.
- Include demonstration setup, expected result, and fallback in notes.

## Accessibility

Verify where supported:

- semantic reading order;
- unique, descriptive slide titles;
- sufficient contrast and readable type;
- alt text or decorative status;
- captions, transcript, and audio description for media;
- table headers and simple table structure;
- keyboard access and equivalent interaction;
- no flashing or unnecessary motion;
- plain language and expanded acronyms;
- equivalent access to content conveyed by color, audio, animation, or spatial position.

Accessibility status must reflect actual verification, not intention.
