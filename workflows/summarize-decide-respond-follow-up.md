# Summarize → Decide → Respond → Follow up

**Audience:** Workplace

## Outcome

A source-supported summary, a clear decision or open question, a response draft, and a follow-up record.

## Before you start

Supply the thread, meeting notes, or document; identify the audience, desired outcome, known decision authority, and any actual deadline. If the source is inaccessible, request its text.

## Steps

1. **Summarize** the source into key points, confirmed decisions, proposed actions, open questions, and risks. Preserve qualifications and attach source labels or brief supporting excerpts.
2. **Decide** what needs attention. Separate decisions already made from recommendations awaiting approval. If evidence or authority is missing, state the decision needed and who must confirm it; do not invent agreement.
3. **Respond** in the appropriate format. Address each request, state the decision or status first, and end with a clear next action. Use only supported facts, owners, deadlines, and commitments. Review the draft before sending.
4. **Follow up** with an action record: task, confirmed owner, due date, status, source, and next checkpoint. Use “Not specified” for absent owners or dates. Resolve those gaps with the responsible person before treating the action as assigned.

## Action boundary

Preparing a draft or action list does not send messages, assign tasks, or create reminders. Perform those actions only when authorized and with the required tools; report what actually completed.

## Example

Fictional notes: “Jordan will review the checklist. Friday launch is proposed.” Output: confirmed action—Jordan reviews the checklist; deadline—Not specified; launch decision—Pending. Draft follow-up asks for the review deadline and launch confirmation.

## Quality check

- Proposals are separate from agreed decisions.
- Actions and commitments trace to the source.
- Missing owners, dates, and authority are explicit.
- Sending, assigning, and scheduling are reported only if actually completed.
